WhatsApp Prescriptions: Are They Legally Valid in India?

Published July 25, 2026 · PediaNex Team

India's Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020) permit registered medical practitioners to issue prescriptions through text-based communication, which covers messaging platforms like WhatsApp, as long as the guidelines' conditions are met — including a valid consultation, proper practitioner and patient identification, and restrictions on certain medicine categories. A valid prescription generally needs the same core elements as a paper one: practitioner name, registration number, signature, patient details, and the medicines prescribed.

Sending a prescription over WhatsApp is now routine in a lot of Indian pediatric practices, but "everyone does it" isn't the same as "it's compliant." Here's what the rules actually cover, and what a clinic should have in place to send a digital prescription with confidence. This is general information for clinic operations, not legal advice — confirm current requirements with the applicable medical council guidance for your situation.

The short version: it's permitted, within limits

India's Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, issued in 2020, formally recognized text-based consultation and prescription as a legitimate mode of practice for registered medical practitioners. That framework is what makes sending a prescription over a messaging app like WhatsApp defensible — it isn't a workaround or a grey area invented by convenience; it's covered by an actual regulatory framework, provided the consultation and prescription meet the guidelines' conditions.

That said, "permitted" comes with conditions, not a blanket allowance. The guidelines set out what has to be true about the consultation, the practitioner's identification, and the type of medicine being prescribed before a text-based prescription is considered valid.

What makes a WhatsApp-sent prescription valid

Practitioner identification

The prescription needs to clearly show the registered practitioner's name, qualification, and registration number, along with a signature — physical, scanned, or digital — the same core requirement as a paper prescription, just delivered through a different channel.

Patient identification and consultation record

The patient's details need to be captured, and the consultation itself should be documented — what was discussed, what was prescribed, and when. This is as much a clinic recordkeeping practice as a legal requirement, and it's what protects both sides if a prescription is ever questioned later.

Medicine category restrictions

Not everything can be prescribed through a text-only consultation. The guidelines restrict certain categories — controlled substances and specific scheduled drug lists — from being prescribed this way, and some situations require an in-person or video consultation before a prescription can be issued at all.

In short: a WhatsApp prescription is legitimate when it carries the same core information a paper prescription would, comes from a documented consultation, and stays within the medicine categories the telemedicine guidelines permit. It's the shortcuts — no registration number, no record of the consultation, prescribing something restricted — that create actual risk, not the channel itself.

Why keeping a record matters as much as sending the message

The compliance conversation tends to focus on whether a prescription can be sent over WhatsApp, but the practical risk for most clinics isn't the sending — it's not having a stored, retrievable copy tied to the patient's record afterward. If a pharmacy questions a dose, a school asks for documentation, or a parent simply loses the chat thread, a clinic that only sent the message and didn't log it has nothing to point back to.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal for a doctor to send a prescription over WhatsApp in India?

India's Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, issued in 2020, permit registered medical practitioners to issue prescriptions through text-based communication, which covers messaging platforms like WhatsApp, provided the consultation and prescription meet the guidelines' conditions. This is general information, not legal advice — clinics should confirm current requirements with the applicable medical council guidance.

What makes a WhatsApp-sent prescription valid?

Under the telemedicine guidelines, a valid prescription generally needs the registered practitioner's name, qualification, registration number, and signature (physical or digital), along with the patient's details and the prescribed medication — the same core elements as a paper prescription, just delivered digitally.

Are there medicines a doctor cannot prescribe over WhatsApp or telemedicine?

Yes. India's telemedicine guidelines restrict certain categories of medicines from being prescribed through a text-only consultation, particularly controlled substances and List O/X category drugs. First-time consultations for some conditions may also require additional steps before certain prescriptions can be issued remotely.

Should a pediatric clinic keep a record of prescriptions sent over WhatsApp?

Yes. Keeping a stored copy of every prescription sent, tied to the patient's record, protects both the clinic and the family if the prescription needs to be referenced later — for a pharmacy, a school, or a future visit.
