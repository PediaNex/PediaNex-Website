Missed a Vaccine Dose? The IAP Catch-Up Schedule Explained

Published July 25, 2026 · PediaNex Team

If a child misses a scheduled vaccine dose, book the next available appointment rather than waiting for the next routine visit. Most IAP-schedule vaccines can be resumed using minimum safe spacing rather than being restarted — the pediatrician calculates the earliest date the missed dose can be safely given. A few vaccines, like rotavirus, have an upper age cutoff for starting the series at all, which is why catching a missed dose early matters more for some vaccines than others.

A missed appointment doesn't mean starting over. The IAP catch-up schedule exists precisely because life happens — illness, travel, a forgotten appointment — and every pediatrician needs a safe way to bring a child back on track. Here's how catch-up dosing actually works, and what a clinic needs in place to catch a missed dose before it turns into a bigger gap.

Catch-up doesn't mean starting over

The most common misconception parents (and sometimes overworked front desks) have is that a missed dose means the whole series restarts. It almost never does. The IAP's Advisory Committee on Vaccines and Immunization Practices (ACVIP) publishes minimum interval rules for exactly this situation — the shortest safe gap allowed between doses of the same vaccine. A catch-up schedule simply recalculates the next dose using those minimum intervals instead of the original ideal-case spacing, so the child gets back on schedule as fast as is safely possible.

This is true for the vast majority of vaccines on the IAP schedule — DTP, hepatitis B, PCV, MMR, and others all have published minimum-interval rules. The exception that matters most in practice is rotavirus vaccine, which has a hard upper age limit for starting the series regardless of how many doses were missed. For nearly everything else, "late" is a schedule adjustment, not a lost opportunity.

What actually happens at a catch-up visit

The pediatrician reviews what's already been given

Every catch-up decision starts with an accurate record of which doses the child has already received and when. This is where a paper card falling out of date, or a parent being unsure exactly which brand or dose was given, causes the most friction — the pediatrician has to reconstruct history before they can plan forward.

Minimum intervals set the new dates

Rather than pushing every future dose back by the length of the delay, the pediatrician applies the minimum allowed spacing between the last dose given and the next one — often shorter than the routine interval — to close the gap as quickly as it's safe to do so.

Age-gated vaccines get flagged first

Rotavirus vaccine in particular is checked immediately: if the child has aged past the cutoff for starting or completing the series, that dose is dropped rather than given late, and the parent is told plainly why.

In short: a missed dose is a scheduling problem, not a medical setback, for almost every vaccine on the IAP list. The one thing that can't be caught up is a vaccine with a hard age cutoff — which is exactly why catching a missed dose within weeks, not months, matters.

Catching a missed dose depends on knowing it was missed in the first place.

Why clinics miss the missed doses

The catch-up rules are well documented; the harder problem in most clinics is simply noticing a child has fallen behind. A paper register only flags a missed dose if someone manually checks that specific child's card against their age — and a front desk juggling a full day of walk-ins rarely has time to audit every open file. The result is that overdue doses often only surface when a parent happens to ask, well after the ideal catch-up window has narrowed.

Frequently asked questions

What is a vaccine catch-up schedule?

A catch-up schedule is a revised dosing plan for a child who has fallen behind the routine IAP immunization timetable. It doesn't restart the series from scratch — it works out the minimum safe intervals to bring the child back on track as quickly and safely as possible.

What should I do if my child misses a vaccine dose?

Book the next available appointment rather than waiting for the original scheduled date to come around again. Most vaccines don't need to be restarted after a delay — the pediatrician will calculate the earliest safe date for the missed dose based on minimum spacing rules.

Do all vaccines allow catch-up dosing?

Most do, but a few — rotavirus vaccine in particular — have an upper age limit for starting the series at all. If that window has passed, the dose may not be given, which is why early, on-time doses matter more for some vaccines than others.

How do clinics track which children are overdue?

Clinics with a digital vaccination register can flag every patient's status against the IAP schedule automatically, based on date of birth and doses already logged, rather than relying on staff to manually check each child's card.
