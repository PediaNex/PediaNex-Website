The IAP Purple Book Explained

Published September 4, 2026 · PediaNex Team

The IAP Purple Book is the Indian Academy of Pediatrics' immunization reference handbook, published by its ACVIP committee. It's a clinical reference for pediatricians, covering vaccine-by-vaccine dosing, contraindications, and special-case guidance in far more depth than the summary schedule chart parents typically see.

Pediatricians and clinic staff sometimes reference "the Purple Book" the way someone might reference a specific edition of a textbook — because in a sense, that's what it is. Here's what's actually in it and how it relates to the schedule most families are handed.

What the Purple Book is

The IAP Purple Book is the Indian Academy of Pediatrics' published immunization handbook — the fuller clinical reference behind the compact schedule chart most parents are familiar with. It's produced through the same ACVIP review process that sets the IAP vaccination schedule, but goes into considerably more depth: vaccine composition, dosing rationale, contraindications, adverse event guidance, and how to handle situations that don't fit neatly into a standard schedule row, like a premature infant or a child with a specific medical condition.

Schedule chart vs. Purple Book

The schedule chart

What most parents and even many clinic wall displays show: a compact, age-by-age list of which vaccine is due when. It answers "what's next" quickly, which is exactly what it's designed for.

The Purple Book

The clinical reference behind that chart — why a dose is timed the way it is, what to do for a child who doesn't fit the standard case, and the detail a pediatrician needs but a parent-facing chart doesn't include.

In short: the schedule chart is the "what," and the Purple Book is the "why and what-if." Clinics use the chart for day-to-day due-date tracking and the handbook as the reference for anything that needs clinical judgment beyond a standard due date.

Where digital tracking fits in

A digital vaccination tracker doesn't replace the Purple Book — it automates the part of the workflow the chart already summarizes: calculating each child's due dates, flagging overdue doses, and keeping the record accurate as the schedule itself gets revised. The Purple Book remains the reference for the clinical judgment calls a due-date calculation can't make on its own — a child with a delayed start, a specific medical condition, or a dosing question that needs a pediatrician's read of the guidance, not just a calendar.

Frequently asked questions

What is the IAP Purple Book?

The IAP Purple Book is the Indian Academy of Pediatrics' immunization reference handbook, published by its ACVIP committee. It goes beyond the summary schedule that parents see, covering vaccine-by-vaccine detail — dosing, contraindications, special situations, and the evidence behind each recommendation — as a reference for pediatricians.

Is the IAP Purple Book meant for parents or for doctors?

It's primarily a clinical reference for pediatricians and healthcare providers, written at a level of technical detail beyond what a parent-facing schedule chart covers. Parents are usually given the simplified schedule, not the handbook itself.

How is the Purple Book different from the IAP vaccination schedule chart?

The schedule chart is a compact, age-by-age summary of what vaccine is due when. The Purple Book is the fuller reference behind that chart — the reasoning, dosing detail, and special-case guidance a pediatrician might need beyond the summary.

Does a clinic need the Purple Book if it already tracks the IAP schedule digitally?

Digital schedule tracking automates when a dose is due; the Purple Book is still the reference for clinical judgment calls — dosing in special situations, contraindications, catch-up nuances — that a due-date tracker isn't designed to answer.
