Published September 4, 2026 · PediaNex Team

Below is a compact, age-by-age summary of the IAP vaccination schedule you can print (Ctrl/Cmd+P) or screenshot directly from this page. It mirrors the same structure as the official IAP schedule. For the full detail on every vaccine, see the complete schedule guide linked below — and always confirm an individual child's due dates with their pediatrician.

Sometimes what's actually needed is just a clean, print-ready table to stick on the fridge — not another long article. Here it is.

Why a printed copy still needs a digital backup

A printed or screenshotted schedule is a handy quick reference, but it's a snapshot frozen at whatever date it was printed — it won't update if the ACVIP revises the schedule, and it won't calculate a specific child's actual due dates based on their date of birth or catch-up status. That's the gap a clinic's digital record fills: a printed chart is for a parent's fridge, and a tracked, per-child digital schedule is what a clinic actually relies on to avoid missed or outdated doses.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official printable PDF of the IAP vaccination schedule 2026?

The Indian Academy of Pediatrics publishes the official schedule through its own channels and immunization handbook. The printable chart on this page mirrors the same age-by-age structure for quick reference and can be printed or screenshotted directly from a browser.

How often should a printed vaccination schedule be replaced?

Whenever the IAP revises the schedule — which happens periodically as new vaccines or evidence emerge. A printed chart from a few years ago can be quietly out of date, which is why clinics increasingly rely on a digital, per-child schedule that reflects the current version rather than a static printout.

Can I print this schedule directly from the browser?

Yes — most browsers' built-in print function (Ctrl+P or Cmd+P) will print this page's schedule table cleanly. A screenshot works just as well for saving a personal copy.

Should a printed schedule be used instead of a doctor's individual guidance?

No. A printed schedule is a general reference. A child's actual due dates should be confirmed with their pediatrician, especially for a child who started late, missed doses, or has a specific medical situation the standard schedule doesn't directly address.
