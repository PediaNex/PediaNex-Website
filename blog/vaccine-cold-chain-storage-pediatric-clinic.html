Vaccine Cold Chain in a Pediatric Clinic: Storage Guide

Published September 17, 2026 · PediaNex Team

Short answer: Vaccines in a pediatric clinic should be stored between +2°C and +8°C in a dedicated pharmaceutical or ice-lined refrigerator, never in a domestic fridge door or freezer compartment. Check and log the temperature at least twice a day with a min-max thermometer. Freeze-sensitive vaccines such as hepatitis B, pentavalent/hexavalent, DTP-containing, IPV, PCV and HPV must never freeze; if freezing is suspected, a shake test identifies damaged adsorbed vaccines. Discard any vial whose vaccine vial monitor (VVM) has reached its discard point.

A vaccine that looks perfect in the vial can be useless if it froze overnight. Cold chain failures are invisible, which is why they come down to routine: the right fridge, a log that's actually filled in, and a plan for power cuts.

Setting up the fridge

  • Use a dedicated fridge for vaccines only: no food, no samples.
  • Keep vaccines in the middle shelves, in original boxes, with space for air to circulate.
  • Nothing in the door. Door shelves swing the most in temperature.
  • Water bottles on the bottom shelf help hold temperature during power cuts.
  • Place the thermometer probe with the vaccines, not near the cooling element.

Freeze-sensitive vs heat-sensitive

Freezing damagesHeat damages most
Hepatitis B, DTwP/DTaP combinations (penta/hexavalent), Td/Tdap, IPV, PCV, HPV, Hib, hepatitis A, influenzaOPV, MMR/MR, varicella, rotavirus, BCG (live vaccines)

Most private-clinic cold chain failures are freezing, not heat: a fridge set too cold, or vials pushed against the back wall.

The shake test, briefly

For adsorbed vaccines you suspect froze: shake the suspect vial alongside a control vial of the same batch that you deliberately froze and thawed. If the suspect vial's sediment settles as slowly as a never-frozen vial, it's likely usable; if it settles as fast as the frozen control, discard it. Follow your state immunization officer's SOP.

Power-cut plan

  1. Keep the fridge door shut; a full fridge holds temperature for hours.
  2. Have a cold box with conditioned ice packs ready.
  3. Use an inverter or UPS for the vaccine fridge specifically.
  4. Log the incident with the min-max reading.
Tracking tip: record brand, batch number and expiry for every dose you give. If a batch is ever recalled or a fridge excursion is found, you can identify exactly which children to call back.

Frequently asked questions

What temperature should vaccines be stored at in a clinic?

Most vaccines should be stored between +2°C and +8°C in a dedicated refrigerator, with the temperature checked and logged at least twice a day.

Which vaccines are damaged by freezing?

Aluminium-adsorbed and inactivated vaccines such as hepatitis B, DTP-containing combinations, Td/Tdap, IPV, PCV, HPV, Hib and hepatitis A are damaged by freezing.

Can I store vaccines in a regular home fridge?

It is not recommended. Domestic fridges swing widely in temperature, especially in the door and near the freezer; a pharmaceutical or ice-lined refrigerator is safer.

What is a vaccine vial monitor (VVM)?

A VVM is a heat-sensitive label on a vial that darkens with cumulative heat exposure; once the inner square matches or is darker than the outer circle, the vaccine should not be used.
